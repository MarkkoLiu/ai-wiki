## Section 2 — Other countries

**United States: proposed AI Agent Accountability Act (announced 1 October).** Senators Josh Hawley and Chris Murphy announced the bipartisan proposal. Per the senators' release, it would apply civil and criminal liability under the Computer Fraud and Abuse Act to an operator that knowingly runs an AI agent that recklessly causes hacking damage or loss, and would expose developers to civil and criminal liability if they fail to implement reasonable safeguards despite knowing, or having reason to know, an agent has hacking capabilities. The US attorney general and state attorneys general could seek injunctions. Full bill text was not published or formally introduced at the time of reporting, so scope and prospects are unresolved (source: 2026-10-02-ai-agent-identity-news.md, citing [Senator Josh Hawley — Senators Hawley, Murphy Announce Bipartisan AI Agent Accountability Act](https://www.hawley.senate.gov/senators-hawley-murphy-announce-bipartisan-ai-agent-accountability-act/) — primary source — and [Axios — Sens. Hawley, Murphy push AI liability as Trump backs self-regulation](https://www.axios.com/2026/10/01/hawley-murphy-ai-liability-trump) — secondary reporting). The report distinguishes it from the earlier Stop Rogue AI Act (standards, inventories, provenance, logs). See [[united-states-ai-agent-policy]] and [[agent-authorization-and-delegation]].
