## Section 1 — Estonia

- **Agent-code bill on the 12 November government-session agenda (11 Nov 2026, 17:52; primary government source — annotated session agenda)**: item 6 lists the draft amendment to Public Information Act § 43¹⁰ on agent-code registration. The explanation describes a unique code linked to a person, identifying when an AI agent acts on that person's behalf, supporting transparency, security and accountability (source: 2026-11-12-ai-agent-identity-news.md, citing [Government of Estonia — Annotated agenda for the 12 November session, item 6](https://www.valitsus.ee/uudised/valitsuse-121126-istungi-kommenteeritud-paevakord)).
- **Status**: the page itself says the agenda was unconfirmed and could change. It established scheduled consideration — not approval, submission to the Riigikogu, enactment or an operational registry — and not that the code grants authority or that agents receive an isikukood (source: 2026-11-12-ai-agent-identity-news.md).
- **Progression**: moves the proposal on from the 10 November cabinet *discussion* ([[2026-11-11-ai-agent-identity-news]]). **Later update**: the 13 November report records government approval of the bill, under a revised title — see [[2026-11-13-ai-agent-identity-news]] and [[estonia-ai-agent-id]].

No other relevant, non-duplicate Estonia source was verified (source: 2026-11-12-ai-agent-identity-news.md).

## Section 2 — Other countries

- **United States — United Airlines restricts unauthorized personal AI agents (11 Nov 2026; primary corporate announcement, corroborated by Reuters at 14:25 EST)**: United is restricting unauthorized third-party personal AI agents and assistants, both directly on its digital platforms and indirectly via travel agencies and aggregators, and is updating booking, ticketing and distribution policies. It cites credential sharing, unintended purchases or account changes, frequent-flyer misuse and automated check-in where human acknowledgment is required, and plans customer guidance through its Digital Trust Center (source: 2026-11-12-ai-agent-identity-news.md, citing [United — United Restricts Access for Third-Party AI Agents](https://united.mediaroom.com/news-releases?item=125496), primary, and [Reuters via StreetInsider](https://www.streetinsider.com/Reuters/United%2BAirlines%2Bmoves%2Bto%2Bblock%2BAI%2Bagents%2Bfrom%2Bits%2Bplatform%2Bover%2Bsecurity%2Bconcerns/27327818.html), secondary).
  - **Status**: an airline restriction, not an FAA or DOT rule; no approved-agent scheme or complete enforcement timetable identified; the regulatory-risk rationale is United's. It extends the service-provider access disputes already recorded for retail (Amazon/Meta, 2026-09-22) and banking (2026-10-25): user delegation does not by itself establish the service's permission. See [[united-states-ai-agent-policy]] and [[agent-authorization-and-delegation]].
- **United Kingdom — Bank of England FPC member on unique agent identity and constrained authority (11 Nov 2026; primary official speech, no hour)**: Jonathan Hall argues alignment alone is insufficient; firms need access controls, least-privilege sandboxes, secure credentials and scalable monitoring. He links unique identities to audit trails and revocation, warns against overly broad or persistent permissions beyond trade execution, interprets existing UK algorithm-identification requirements as applying naturally to AI trading agents, and notes firm-level controls do not remove financial-stability risk from concentrated, correlated agent behaviour (source: 2026-11-12-ai-agent-identity-news.md, citing [Bank of England — Principals and Agents, Jonathan Hall](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/speech/2026/november/jon-hall-speech-at-cf-imperial-workshop)).
  - **Status**: policy discussion, not a new identity rule or binding supervisory standard; the interpretation of existing trading rules is Hall's. See [[united-kingdom-ai-agent-policy]] and [[ai-agent-identity]].
