## Section 2 — Other countries

- **Papua New Guinea — revised draft National AI Policy sets limits on government agents (release announced 26 Nov; primary)**: the Department of Information and Communications Technology's draft would require government agents to be **identifiable**, have a **named human owner**, and operate with a defined purpose and **documented permissions**; keep delegated actions within the original authority; require human approval for high-impact or out-of-scope actions; require audit records and a way to stop connected agents and revoke access; and assign responsibility for agent actions to the **deploying organisation**. A proposed initial government pilot would require explicit user approval before each external action or change to an official record. Consultation details to follow (source: 2026-11-27-ai-agent-identity-news.md, citing [DICT release announcement](https://www.ict.gov.pg/dict-sets-direction-for-safe-responsible-use-of-artificail-intelligence-with-revised-national-ai-policy/) and [Revised National AI Policy consultation draft, pp. 15–16 and 21](https://www.ict.gov.pg/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/PNG_National_AI_Policy_Revised_09Nov2026-1.pdf), primary). **Status**: consultation draft, not adopted policy. First PNG item in the wiki — new page [[papua-new-guinea-ai-agent-policy]].
- **United Kingdom — Bank of England FPC record on traceable identity and bounded agent authority (published 26 Nov; meeting 19 Nov; primary)**: the Financial Policy Committee says consumer agents need clear instructions and limits on delegated authority, with testing and monitoring of whether actions match users' intentions; calls for rapid attention to accountability, liability and redress, and **traceability of which agent acted, for whom and with what permissions**. It warns individually intended actions can create destabilising collective effects (faster deposit movements, investment reallocations) and highlights access controls, monitoring, effective human involvement and system-wide safeguards (source: 2026-11-27-ai-agent-identity-news.md, citing [Bank of England — FPC Record, November 2026](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/financial-policy-committee-record/2026/november-2026), primary, esp. paras 41 and 57–58). **Status**: financial-stability assessment — not a registry, liability rule or identity specification. Committee-level follow-on to FPC member Jonathan Hall's 11 Nov speech ([[2026-11-12-ai-agent-identity-news]]); no contradiction.
- **United Kingdom — HM Treasury speech supports the agentic-payments policy sprint (delivered 25 Nov, published 26 Nov; primary)**: Economic Secretary Lucy Rigby names identity, consent, fraud, responsibility and liability as central questions for autonomous payments, and backs government/FCA/industry collaboration through the **Agentic Policy Sprint** with clear accountability and early regulatory engagement (source: 2026-11-27-ai-agent-identity-news.md, citing [HM Treasury — Economic Secretary's TechUK speech](https://www.gov.uk/government/speeches/economic-secretary-to-the-treasury-speech-at-techuk), primary). The report counts it as supporting coverage, not a new launch or legislation. Relates to the FCA sprint recorded 2026-11-25 and Treasury's 2026-11-18 liability remarks on [[united-kingdom-ai-agent-policy]].

No other qualifying, non-duplicate country-level development was verified (source: 2026-11-27-ai-agent-identity-news.md).
