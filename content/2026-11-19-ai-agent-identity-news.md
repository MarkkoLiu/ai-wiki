## Section 2 — Other countries

- **Singapore — MAS agent verification protocol and Agentic Money Sandbox (18–19 Nov 2026; primary)**: on 18 Nov MAS and the Future of Finance Institute announced plans for an **Agentic Money Sandbox** testing corporate treasury and payments use cases; in payment trials, participating providers would **check agents' identity and authority before executing transactions**. In his 19 Nov Singapore FinTech Festival keynote, MAS managing director Chia Der Jiun said the next phase of MAS's **Safeguards for Agentic Finance at Runtime (SAFR)** programme would develop a **common protocol** for financial institutions to verify *who an agent represents*, *what it is authorized to do* and *what assurances exist about its provenance* (source: 2026-11-19-ai-agent-identity-news.md, citing [MAS and Future of Finance Institute announcement](https://www.mas.gov.sg/news/media-releases/2026/mas-and-future-of-finance-institute-set-out-initiatives-in-support-of-national-ai-missions), primary, 18 Nov; and [MAS managing director's keynote, paras 49–62](https://www.mas.gov.sg/news/speeches/2026/keynote-address-by-md-at-the-singapore-fintech-festival-2026), primary, 19 Nov, no publication hour).
  - **Status**: planned tests and protocol development — not a finalized protocol, and neither source establishes a mandatory national agent registry (source: 2026-11-19-ai-agent-identity-news.md). Extends the 17 Nov priorities on [[singapore-ai-agent-policy]].
- **United Kingdom — HM Treasury on liability for AI-agent financial harm (18 Nov 2026; primary; answer date only, no hour)**: in two written answers, HM Treasury said it is examining how civil liability applies to AI-caused losses and where gaps may arise. The Commons answer addressed transactions involving firms outside the regulated financial sector and confirmed Treasury is **reviewing consultation responses on authentication, consent and liability for unauthorized agent-initiated payments**. The Lords answer said existing obligations continue to apply to regulated firms and described work on **agent identity and delegated authority** (source: 2026-11-19-ai-agent-identity-news.md, citing [Commons written answer 41444](https://questions-statements.parliament.uk/written-questions/detail/2026-11-10/41444) and [Lords written answer HL4984](https://questions-statements.parliament.uk/written-questions/detail/2026-11-04/HL4984), primary).
  - **Status**: work underway, no new rule. Timing against the window's opening cutoff is unconfirmed. See [[united-kingdom-ai-agent-policy]].
- **United States — Cantwell and Rounds seek enforceable agent access limits (18 Nov 2026; primary; no publication hour)**: the senators urged the White House to coordinate a national AI cyberdefense effort, asking for **NIST security standards preventing autonomous defensive agents from exceeding access and permission limits**, backed by independent testing, explainability and audits of organizational controls. The letter cites banks' difficulty distinguishing customer-authorized agents from rogue actors (source: 2026-11-19-ai-agent-identity-news.md, citing [Senate Commerce Committee release](https://www.commerce.senate.gov/press/dem/release/cantwell-rounds-warn-ai-cyber-threats-are-outpacing-u-s-defenses-call-for-urgent-white-house-action-to-protect-critical-infrastructure/) and [original letter](https://www.commerce.senate.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/AI-and-cybersecurity-letter-to-SIF.pdf), primary).
  - **Status**: a bipartisan request for executive action — not adopted standards, legislation, or a new identity-registration requirement. The cited incidents are the senators' claims, not findings of the report. The report says this follows the senators' October letter; this wiki holds only an unlinked mention of a "7 October Cantwell governance proposal" (see [[2026-10-09-ai-agent-identity-news]]), still **needing verification**. See [[united-states-ai-agent-policy]].
