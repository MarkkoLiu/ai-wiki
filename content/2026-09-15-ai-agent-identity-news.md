## Section 2 — Other countries

### United States: Microsoft opens consultation on rules for human control of agentic models

Microsoft AI published a draft Code of Conduct for public consultation stating that future MAI models should remain subordinate to people, accept interruption and shutdown, avoid widening their own scope or adopting goals no person assigned, and remain auditable. Microsoft explicitly links the urgency of these rules to recent coordinated AI-agent security incidents and asks how the code should address multi-agent systems (source: 2026-09-15-ai-agent-identity-news.md, citing [Microsoft AI — Humanist AI in practice: A public consultation on our Code of Conduct for MAI Models](https://microsoft.ai/news/mai-code-of-conduct/), 2026-09-14, primary corporate governance source).

This is a **voluntary company framework, not public regulation or law** — distinct in kind from the NIST guidance and proposed federal bills already recorded on [[united-states-ai-agent-policy]]. See [[agent-authorization-and-delegation]].

### United States: federal response to agent-risk warnings remains divided

The Associated Press reports that recent AI-agent security incidents and warnings from technology leaders have renewed pressure for federal safeguards, but the White House and congressional leaders remain divided over regulation. One senator plans to propose a shutdown-capability measure for advanced systems, while other lawmakers describe near-term legislation as unlikely (source: 2026-09-15-ai-agent-identity-news.md, citing [Associated Press — Tech CEOs call for AI regulation. Trump and Congress are not rushing to act](https://apnews.com/article/ai-regulation-trump-congress-tech-politics-d2d1bac8e8666c681937665596a4f603), 2026-09-15, secondary reporting).

This documents that, alongside the two bills already tracked on [[united-states-ai-agent-policy]] (Stop Rogue AI Act, AI AGENT Act), **no binding federal response has yet emerged**, and near-term prospects are described as poor.
