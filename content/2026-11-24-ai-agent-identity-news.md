## Section 1 — Estonia

- **Bill 1092 SE sent to first reading (primary)**: the Riigikogu register now lists the agent-code bill (supplementing the Public Information Act — agent code) as **sent to first reading**; the Constitutional Committee had scheduled preparation for that reading at its 23 November meeting at 11:15. The bill would create a person-linked code so services can tell when an AI agent acts on someone's behalf (source: 2026-11-24-ai-agent-identity-news.md, citing [Riigikogu bill register](https://www.riigikogu.ee/tegevus/eelnoud/3257efe0-f7c9-42c1-bf63-bb9b4c5188ec/avaliku-teabe-seaduse-taiendamise-seadus-agendikood/), primary, and [Riigikogu advance notice for 23 November](https://www.riigikogu.ee/pressiteated/riigikogu-eelinfo-esmaspaevaks-23-novembriks-3/), primary, published 22 Nov).
  - **Status**: a parliamentary-process step. The report states this does **not** establish that the plenary first reading has taken place, that the bill has passed, or that agent codes are operational. The advance notice describes a planned committee meeting, not its minutes; the time the register changed is unavailable (source: 2026-11-24-ai-agent-identity-news.md).
  - **Consistency**: progression from "accepted into proceedings" (recorded 2026-11-18 to 2026-11-23), not a contradiction. The 2026-09-08 isikukood correction, 2026-10-25 agendikood position and 2026-11-16 open questions all stand. See [[estonia-ai-agent-id]].

## Section 2 — Other countries

- **United Kingdom — written answer HL5114 on agent hallucinations (answered 23 Nov 2026; primary)**: Baroness Lloyd of Effra says existing legal and regulatory frameworks apply to AI agents, including where inaccurate or misleading outputs cause harm; whether liability arises depends on the facts, applicable law and the parties' responsibilities (source: 2026-11-24-ai-agent-identity-news.md, citing [UK Parliament — HL5114](https://questions-statements.parliament.uk/written-questions/detail/2026-11-09/HL5114), primary).
  - **Status**: a clarification, not a new liability regime, strict-liability rule or legal personality for agents. Adds to the HM Treasury answers (2026-11-18) and JCHR response (2026-11-20). See [[united-kingdom-ai-agent-policy]].
- **United Arab Emirates — federal AI concierge Mira (23 Nov 2026; primary + secondary)**: the government unveiled an AI concierge to help users complete public-service transactions in conversation; ministers demonstrated passport, Emirates ID and residency renewals; users can review details and confirm requests within the conversation where the service allows; first services scheduled for **March 2027** (source: 2026-11-24-ai-agent-identity-news.md, citing [Dubai Protocol Department](https://www.protocol.dubai.ae/en/media-listing/news-events/mohammed-bin-rashid-witnesses-launch-of-new-uae-government-services-model-ai-concierge-agent-mira/), primary, and [The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/uae/2026/11/23/uae-unveils-government-ai-assistant-called-mira-to-boost-public-services/), secondary).
  - **Status**: an announced rollout, not a live nationwide agent service or a new legal delegation standard (source: 2026-11-24-ai-agent-identity-news.md). See [[united-arab-emirates-ai-agent-policy]].
- **International standards — OAuth 2.0 over the Agent Transfer Protocol (announced 23 Nov 2026, 18:02 UTC; primary individual Internet-Draft)**: Chris Hood proposes binding an OAuth token to the agent that can prove control of the corresponding key; a subagent needing direct access would obtain its own token via an exchange that checks the delegation chain (source: 2026-11-24-ai-agent-identity-news.md, citing [IETF Datatracker](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/draft-hood-oauth-over-agtp/) and [IETF publication notice](https://mailarchive.ietf.org/arch/msg/i-d-announce/Q8920rfXTc-vn0Ba9LhszZkvVDA/), primary).
  - **Status**: an individual submission, not an IETF-endorsed standard or RFC. See [[agent-standards-and-interoperability]].
- **United States — Bank Policy Institute on agent access to accounts (23 Nov 2026; secondary, with 20 Nov primary background)**: Inside AI Policy covered a BPI analysis recommending banks block agent access through unapproved channels and warn customers about potential losses, arguing current safeguards leave banks exposed to mistaken or fraudulent transactions and actions outside the customer's intended scope (source: 2026-11-24-ai-agent-identity-news.md, citing [Inside AI Policy](https://insideaipolicy.com/ai-daily-news/bank-policy-institute-warns-insufficient-controls-around-ai-agents-seeking-access), secondary, and [BPI analysis](https://bpi.com/ai-agents-should-not-access-financial-accounts-just-yet/), primary, 20 Nov).
  - **Status**: an industry policy group's position, newly covered; not a rule or regulator decision. See [[united-states-ai-agent-policy]].

The report excluded Wells Fargo's 24 November effective-date terms as already covered (see [[2026-10-25-ai-agent-identity-news]]). No other qualifying development was verified (source: 2026-11-24-ai-agent-identity-news.md).
