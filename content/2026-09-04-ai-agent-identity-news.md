# AI Agent Identity and Personal Code News (2026-09-04 baseline)

**Summary**: First-run baseline digest of global coverage on digital identities and personal identification codes for AI agents, covering Estonia, the United States, China, Singapore, and Malaysia.

**Sources**: 2026-09-04-ai-agent-identity-news.md

**Last updated**: 2026-09-04

---

This is a first-run baseline note. It covers recent reporting on [[ai-agent-identity]], personal identification codes, [[agent-authorization-and-delegation|delegated authority, authorization, and accountability]], and related governance, current through 2026-09-04. Future updates to this topic will cover only newly published sources and will exclude duplicate stories already logged here.

## Coverage summary

- **Estonia** is the lead story: the Eesti.ai advisory council agreed to advance an "AI personal identification code" for agents acting on behalf of people, companies, or institutions (source: 2026-09-04-ai-agent-identity-news.md). See [[estonia-ai-agent-id]].
- **United States**: NIST guidance argues agents need first-class identities bound to a responsible user, built on existing patterns like OAuth 2.0 and SPIFFE (source: 2026-09-04-ai-agent-identity-news.md). See [[united-states-ai-agent-policy]].
- **China**: reportedly introduced a national standard for AI-agent interconnection with unified identity management and identity-code provisions (source: 2026-09-04-ai-agent-identity-news.md). See [[china-ai-agent-policy]].
- **Singapore**: a government sandbox with Google identified gaps in oversight, identity/authentication frameworks, and permission controls for agents (source: 2026-09-04-ai-agent-identity-news.md). See [[singapore-ai-agent-policy]].
- **Malaysia**: commentary frames autonomous agents as "non-human identities" needing their own access rights and a clearer legal accountability framework (source: 2026-09-04-ai-agent-identity-news.md). See [[malaysia-ai-agent-policy]].
