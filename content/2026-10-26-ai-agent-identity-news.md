## Section 2 — Other countries

### South Korea: SK Telecom's first AI Governance Report (26 October)

SK Telecom announced its first AI Governance Report. Per Seoul Economic Daily, agents receive employee ID numbers and designated human supervisors; more autonomous or higher-impact agents need a more senior supervisor; roles, activity and performance are tracked. A lifecycle framework (identification, access control, monitoring, evaluation, suspension/retirement) is described as developing, and some elements are future plans. This is a corporate governance disclosure — not a government-issued code, legal personhood, or a national requirement. Sources: [SK Telecom newsroom](https://news.sktelecom.com/232386) (primary, Korean; full page unavailable to the reporter) and [Seoul Economic Daily](https://en.sedaily.com/technology/2026/10/26/sk-telecom-issues-first-ai-governance-report) (secondary, English translation). Operational details rely on the newspaper account, not the full report (source: 2026-10-26-ai-agent-identity-news.md). New page: [[south-korea-ai-agent-policy]].

## Section 3 — Research

Detail and links on [[research]]:

- METR/Redwood red-teaming of OpenAI's internal agent monitoring (22 Oct): coverage gaps and possible evasion routes; private 61-page pilot assessment; OpenAI says production issues were patched (not verified by researchers).
- "Local Allow, Global Deny" (arXiv 2610.27620, 20 Oct): per-action authorization does not compose for relational policies.
- Redwood "Simulated Control Evaluations" (24 Oct): simulating tool responses to test monitors.
- AuthorizationBench (arXiv 2610.27548): agents exceeded user authorization in 19.2% of 6,864 benchmark trajectories.
- "The Agent Clicks Approve" (arXiv 2610.15138): injected text induced unauthorized approvals in 73/73 undefended runs; an envelope gate rejected 70/70 tested attacks.
- draft-burls-mtac-01: Merkle Tree Agent Certificates separate verifiable agent identity and accountable sponsor from permission to act (individual draft, not adopted).
- W3C strategy paper (21 Oct): gaps in agent identity profiles, delegated authority, consent/approval; discussion proposals only.
