## Section 2 — Other countries

### United Kingdom: healthcare AI recommendations

On 10 September 2026, the National Commission into the Regulation of AI in Healthcare published recommendations for a future UK healthcare-AI regulatory framework, covering accountability, transparency, organisational governance, and system-wide assurance (source: 2026-09-11-ai-agent-identity-news.md, citing [MHRA / GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/national-commission-into-the-regulation-of-ai-in-healthcare-recommendations-for-a-future-regulatory-framework), primary government source). It is broader than agent identity, but relevant to delegated and autonomous AI because it frames accountability as a system-level obligation rather than only a model or vendor issue.

**Note on provenance**: this same "National Commission into the Regulation of AI in Healthcare" item first appeared in the wiki on 2026-09-10, sourced from a since-corrected intermediate version of that day's raw file, and was removed when the corrected file turned out not to contain it (see [[log]], 2026-09-10 entry). Today's report cites the item again — this time with a clickable primary GOV.UK source and an explicit statement that it is a distinct 10 September publication, not a repeat of the 7 September UK incident-response statement. It has accordingly been reinstated on [[united-kingdom-ai-agent-policy]] and [[agent-authorization-and-delegation]]. This is a genuine confirmation of a real story whose earlier citation was unreliable, not a reversal of a correction.

No other country developments were reported today; Australian, Californian, U.S., Singaporean, workplace-agent, EMVCo, OWASP, and authorization-standard items already covered in earlier reports were not repeated (source: 2026-09-11-ai-agent-identity-news.md).
