# Kenya: Government Platform for Authorized Agent Action

**Summary**: Kenya is building a unified government API platform to prepare for AI agents in public-service delivery. The stated control model is bounded, authorized action with audit trails and escalation to human officers (September 2026).

**Sources**: 2026-09-24-ai-agent-identity-news.md

**Last updated**: 2026-09-24

---

## 2026-09-23: unified platform linking government systems

Kenya is building an enterprise platform to connect government systems through secure APIs as it prepares to use AI agents in public-service delivery. Principal Secretary for ICT and the Digital Economy John Tanui described this as a shift toward systems that can pursue **authorized outcomes within defined limits**, while keeping human judgment (source: 2026-09-24-ai-agent-identity-news.md, citing [TechTrendsKE — State plans unified platform to link government systems](https://techtrendske.co.ke/2026/09/23/state-plans-unified-platform-to-link-government-systems/), 2026-09-23, secondary reporting on a government keynote).

The stated control model includes (same source):

- Zero Trust architecture
- explicit data-access rules
- authorization of individual actions
- audit trails
- error handling
- escalation to human officers

This is an implementation direction for bounded delegated authority across public-sector systems, not an agent-identity scheme in the sense of unique identifiers or a registry. In kind, it sits closer to Australia's accountable-human and harness-enforcement guidance ([[australia-ai-agent-policy]]) and Singapore's guidance-led approach ([[singapore-ai-agent-policy]]) than to Estonia's Aruait programme ([[estonia-ai-agent-id]]). See [[agent-authorization-and-delegation]].
