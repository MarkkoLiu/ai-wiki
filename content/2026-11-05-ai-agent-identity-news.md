## Section 2 — Other countries

- **United States — OCC examiner framework (4 Nov; primary)**: OCC news release 2026-97 and Bulletin 2026-54 give examiners a framework for banks' use of AI and agentic systems, tying risk assessment to potential consequences, transfer of agency and human oversight; control intensity can rise with the consequences of agent failure or misuse. Examiners should not treat the technology as a separate risk category. The bulletin says it is an examiner resource, not new supervisory expectations or enforceable standards, and introduces no agent-ID mandate. The publications use "Super Intelligence" for what was previously "Artificial Intelligence"; that terminology does not establish a capability level (source: 2026-11-05-ai-agent-identity-news.md, citing [OCC news release](https://www.occ.gov/news-issuances/news-releases/2026/nr-occ-2026-97.html) and [OCC Bulletin 2026-54](https://www.occ.gov/news-issuances/bulletins/2026/bulletin-2026-54.html)). See [[united-states-ai-agent-policy]].
- **China — CAC intelligent-internet cooperation initiative (4 Nov, 16:00; primary, Chinese-language)**: announced at the Wuzhen summit; calls for open, transparent interoperability standards (interfaces, protocols, semantics, workflows) and a trust framework covering agent discovery, negotiation and execution so cross-platform interactions can be verified and traced; safety provisions address data poisoning and loss of control, with lifecycle security, risk-information sharing and emergency cooperation. A cooperation proposal, not an adopted specification, registry or enforcement mechanism; no personal-identification-code requirement (source: 2026-11-05-ai-agent-identity-news.md, citing [CAC](https://www.cac.gov.cn/2026-11/04/c_1795548985565845.htm); an [official Jiangsu republication](https://jswx.gov.cn/zhengce/fagui/202611/t20261104_1370276.shtml) carries the same text and is not a separate story). See [[china-ai-agent-policy]].
